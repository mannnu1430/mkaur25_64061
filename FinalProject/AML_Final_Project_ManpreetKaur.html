1. Experiment Overview

This report documents the experimental results of training and evaluating two deep learning architectures for binary sentiment classification on the IMDB movie review benchmark. The two models compared are a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory network (BiLSTM) and a compact Transformer encoder. Both models are trained from scratch under identical conditions to isolate the effect of architectural choice from confounding factors such as pre-training data or differential hyperparameter tuning.

The IMDB dataset consists of 50,000 English-language movie reviews, split evenly into 25,000 training and 25,000 test samples. The classification task is binary: reviews rated 7 or higher out of 10 are labeled positive, and reviews rated 4 or lower are labeled negative. The dataset is perfectly balanced, with 50% positive and 50% negative examples in each split.

3. Training Dynamics

Both models were trained for up to 15 epochs with EarlyStopping monitoring validation loss with patience of 3. The BiLSTM converged at epoch 11 and the Transformer at epoch 9. The ReduceLROnPlateau callback fired once during each model's training, halving the learning rate upon detecting a plateau in validation loss, which produced a final incremental improvement before EarlyStopping triggered.

Training Observations

The BiLSTM begins epoch 1 at roughly 62% training accuracy and improves steadily, reaching approximately 91% by its final epoch. Its validation accuracy plateaus near 87.6%, with a generalization gap of roughly 3 percentage points — consistent with mild overfitting that the SpatialDropout and recurrent dropout partially, but not fully, suppress. Training loss falls from approximately 0.68 to 0.23, while validation loss reaches a minimum near 0.30 before rising, triggering EarlyStopping.

The Transformer converges more rapidly: training accuracy starts near 65% and rises steeply to approximately 94% by its final epoch. Validation accuracy peaks near 89.5%, and validation loss reaches a lower minimum of approximately 0.27. The steeper initial learning curve reflects the Transformer's ability to immediately exploit pairwise token attention across the full sequence from the first gradient update.

5. Computational Efficiency

A key finding of this study is that the Transformer achieves superior predictive performance while simultaneously being more efficient in terms of both model size and training time. The figure below summarises these efficiency differences.

Figure 4. Comparison of trainable parameter counts (left) and total training time in seconds under EarlyStopping (right). The Transformer requires 395,712 fewer parameters (31.7% reduction) and trains 87.6 seconds faster (30.8% reduction).

The BiLSTM contains 1,248,641 trainable parameters, while the Transformer contains only 852,929 — a reduction of 395,712 parameters (31.7%). This is counterintuitive given that Transformer architectures are often associated with very large models, but results from the large recurrent weight matrices inherent in stacked Bidirectional LSTM cells. Total training time was 284.3 seconds for the BiLSTM versus 196.7 seconds for the Transformer — a 30.8% reduction attributable to both the parallelism of self-attention computation and the Transformer's earlier convergence.

6. Key Findings

The Transformer encoder outperforms the BiLSTM on every evaluation metric examined while also being more parameter-efficient and faster to train. On the 25,000-sample IMDB test set, the Transformer achieves 89.32% accuracy and a ROC-AUC of 0.9611, compared to 87.63% and 0.9498 for the BiLSTM. The accuracy improvement of +1.69 pp corresponds to a 13.7% reduction in the error rate.

Both models substantially outperform classical machine learning baselines (logistic regression on TF-IDF typically achieves approximately 85% on IMDB) and approach the performance of competitive from-scratch neural architectures reported in the literature. Neither model uses external pre-training; fine-tuned models such as BERT achieve over 95% on the same benchmark.

The Transformer's faster convergence (9 epochs vs. 11 for the BiLSTM) and lower parameter count make it the preferred architecture for this task when training from scratch, both in terms of predictive performance and computational cost.

7. Error Analysis

Examining the confusion matrices, both models produce more false positive errors (negative reviews predicted as positive) than false negative errors (positive reviews predicted as negative). The BiLSTM produces 1,819 false positives and 1,290 false negatives; the Transformer produces 1,579 false positives and 990 false negatives. This asymmetry likely reflects properties of the IMDB dataset: negative reviews frequently employ strong, unambiguous language that is straightforward to detect, while positive reviews can express sentiment in a more nuanced, qualified manner that requires understanding of broader context.

The Transformer reduces both error types relative to the BiLSTM: it produces 300 fewer false negatives and 240 fewer false positives. This balanced improvement suggests that the self-attention mechanism captures sentiment-relevant contextual signals more effectively in both directions, rather than simply shifting the decision boundary.
